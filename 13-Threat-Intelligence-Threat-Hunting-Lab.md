- Learn hypothesis-based threat hunting
- Analyze suspicious behavior and security events
- Understand the threat hunting investigation process

## Threat Intelligence

Threat intelligence was studied as the process of collecting, analyzing, and sharing information about threats, threat actors, indicators, and emerging risks.

Information considered during the exercises included:

- Threat actor or group information
- Locations
- IP addresses
- Email addresses
- Malicious links
- Indicators of compromise
- Attack techniques and behaviors

The purpose is to provide actionable information that can help security teams improve detection, investigation, and defensive controls.

## Threat Intelligence Lifecycle

The threat intelligence lifecycle was studied as a continuous process involving:

1. Planning and direction
2. Collection
3. Processing
4. Analysis
5. Dissemination
6. Feedback

The lifecycle helps transform raw threat data into useful intelligence for security operations.

## Threat Hunting

Threat hunting focuses on proactively searching for threats that may have bypassed existing security controls.

The exercises covered:

- Developing a hunting hypothesis
- Collecting and processing relevant intelligence
- Identifying investigation triggers
- Analyzing suspicious behavior
- Investigating related events
- Determining the scope of activity
- Reaching an investigation outcome

Indicator of Compromise (IOC)

Evidence that may indicate a system or environment has been compromised.

Examples include:

Malicious IP addresses
Malicious domains
File hashes
Suspicious files
Malicious URLs
Indicator of Attack (IOA)

Behavioral evidence associated with an attack or malicious activity.

Examples include:

Repeated authentication failures
Suspicious process activity
Unusual network connections
Abnormal privilege escalation
Indicator of Exposure (IOE)

Information indicating that a system or environment may be exposed to a security risk.
